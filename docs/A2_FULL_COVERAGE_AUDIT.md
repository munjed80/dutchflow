# A2 full coverage audit

Updated 2026-10-07 after merged PR #27. This audit reviews the complete authored A2 sequence as one course: 8 units, 32 lessons, 256 lesson phrases, 128 lesson questions, 192 lexical/form notes, 64 lesson production tasks, 8 readings, 8 guided scenarios and 8 integrated unit packs.

This is a curriculum coverage audit, not CEFR certification. CEFR descriptors are used as design references only. The Council of Europe describes language activity across reception, production, interaction and mediation, and across personal, public, educational and professional domains. DutchFlow must therefore be judged by what learners can do with information, not by lesson counts alone.

## Executive decision

Do **not** add a ninth four-lesson unit now.

The eight-unit sequence already covers the major everyday A2 domains and most of the target interaction patterns. The main remaining weaknesses are cross-unit transfer and a few underrepresented communicative functions, not another topical domain.

Before freezing authored A2, add one cumulative **A2 Final Integrated Review** and make a small set of targeted reinforcement edits. The final review should test unfamiliar information across units without reusing public lesson text.

## What is already strong

### Domain coverage

The sequence spans all four practical CEFR domains in a useful Netherlands-oriented way:

| Domain | Strongest DutchFlow evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Personal | recent activities, weekend visits, social experiences, invitations |
| Public | shopping, travel, local forms/messages, neighbourhood activities |
| Educational | school instructions, deadlines, replies, clarification |
| Professional | work progress, delays, instructions, handover |

Housing/services and appointments add important everyday transactional coverage that cuts across personal/public contexts.

### Reception

Reading progresses from 73 words in unit 1 to a peak of 126 words in unit 7. Every unit has an original reading with evidence-backed questions. The materials already require learners to distinguish details that are easy to confuse: deadline versus event time, availability versus reservation, departure versus arrival, proposal versus confirmation, and original information versus later updates.

Integrated listening remains controlled at 44–63 words per unit. This is appropriate for clear A2 input, but the length progression is almost flat and all eight sources are essentially direct personal/task messages rather than a broader mixture of message and public-announcement formats.

### Interaction

Interaction is one of the strongest parts of the course. The 8 scenarios each contain 4 turns plus a transfer task. Across the sequence learners must:

- ask follow-up questions;
- ask for clarification;
- explain a delay or conflict;
- compare alternatives;
- make or respond to a proposal;
- confirm a final plan;
- correct changed details;
- request missing information;
- distinguish a request or proposal from an accepted arrangement.

Formal and informal address are both present. Units 3–6 use formal service-style language heavily; units 1–2 and 7–8 provide more informal peer interaction.

### Production and writing

All 32 lessons have two open production tasks. Model responses generally stay short enough for A2 and move beyond recognition. Later tasks demand more information: reasons, changed details, comparison, a proposed alternative, or a final confirmation.

The course already contains relevant short-message writing in work, appointments, school/local messages, travel and invitations. This is a solid A2 base.

### Grammar recycling

The course avoids a grammar-dump structure. Important patterns recur across later units:

- perfect tense starts in unit 1 and reappears in work, housing, appointments and social experience;
- reasons with `want`, `omdat` and `daarom` recur from unit 2 onward;
- comparisons appear in housing, appointments, shopping and travel;
- obligation / no-need language appears in work, appointments, school and travel;
- proposal/acceptance patterns recur in work, appointments, travel and social plans;
- `u` and `je` are both recycled in appropriate contexts.

The lexical repetitions found in the audit are mostly useful recycling rather than accidental duplication. Examples include `afgesproken`, `passen`, `meenemen`, `afspreken`, `aankomen`, `met wie` and `de ingang`.

## Coverage risks

### 1. No cumulative A2 transfer task — high priority

A2 has 8 separate unit packs but no final cumulative pack equivalent to A1's final review.

This means the repository can show that learners can solve eight locally-scaffolded situations, but it does not yet show that they can transfer the same language when the domain and source change.

**Required fix:** create one final integrated review that deliberately combines skills and information from multiple units.

Recommended shape:

1. Read a short practical notice or message with 3–4 facts.
2. Hear a later update that changes at least two of those facts.
3. Identify which information is still valid.
4. Write a short message to a third person relaying the current information.
5. Complete a partner task that requires one clarification question, one reason, one proposal and final confirmation.

This should not produce an A2 score or certificate.

### 2. Mediation / relaying information is underrepresented — high priority

Learners frequently respond to information, but they rarely have to **relay** the useful part of one source to another person.

Examples of the missing performance:
- “The school message says we must reply by Tuesday; tell your partner what they need to do.”
- “The station announcement changes the platform; tell your friend the new plan.”
- “The repair company sent two time windows; explain the useful option to a family member.”

The final integrated review should contain an explicit relay task. A small number of existing production tasks can later be adjusted to reinforce this without adding a new unit.

### 3. Listening source variety is too narrow — medium/high priority

Unit listening passages are all short and clear, but they are mostly message/update speech directed to one person. A2 learners should also practise extracting the main point and key details from short clear announcements or public information.

**Required fix:** include at least one short public announcement in the final review, such as:
- a station/platform change;
- a community-centre schedule change;
- a clinic or school waiting-room announcement.

Keep it fictional and avoid testing specialist vocabulary.

### 4. Too much success can come from “proposal → alternative → confirmation” patterns — medium priority

This pattern is pedagogically useful and appropriately recycled, but it is highly represented in appointments, travel and social planning. The risk is that learners become good at the course's preferred interaction template without equivalent exposure to other A2 discourse moves.

The final review should therefore require:
- summarising/relaying a fact;
- asking for clarification;
- giving a reason;
- correcting outdated information;
- then confirming an action.

Do not add more standalone lessons whose main objective is simply changing a time and confirming it.

### 5. Listening difficulty does not clearly rise across the level — medium priority

Reading shows a visible increase in density (73 → 85 → 93 → 98 → 105/104 → 126 words before unit 8 returns to 101). Listening remains approximately 44–63 words throughout.

Length alone is not a proficiency measure, so this is not automatically a defect. However, the later listening tasks should become harder through discourse demands: one correction, one irrelevant detail, a contrast between old/new information, or a required inference from two facts.

The unit 7 and 8 update tasks already move in this direction. The final review should complete the progression through integration rather than simply longer audio.

### 6. Opinion and preference are present but less distributed than reasons/comparisons — medium priority

Explicit personal evaluation is concentrated in appointments/choices, travel and social experience. This is adequate for a preview, but the final review should ask for a simple preference plus a reason in an unfamiliar context.

No separate “opinions” unit is justified.

### 7. Indirect questions are intentionally narrow — low priority

The explicit indirect-question pattern appears mainly in lesson 23. At A2 this is acceptable if kept formulaic. It should not be expanded into a grammar block. One final-review clarification such as `Kunt u zeggen of ...?` is enough to verify transfer.

## Progression audit

| Stage | Main cognitive/communicative demand | Audit result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Units 1–2 | report events/tasks, order information, ask follow-up/clarification | strong foundation |
| Units 3–4 | compare states/options, handle service windows, reasons, follow-up | appropriate increase |
| Units 5–6 | quantities/conditions, obligations, deadlines, relevant written response | strong transactional A2 |
| Units 7–8 | multi-step plans, updates, alternative choice, social turn-taking | strong interaction; some overlap |
| Whole level | transfer between unrelated domains and relay current information | **missing cumulative evidence** |

The course therefore has a reasonable unit progression but lacks a final cross-domain transfer gate.

## Recommended final-review blueprint

Create `a2-final-review` as a cumulative practice pack covering all 8 unit IDs.

Suggested fictional situation:

**Community workshop trip**
- Written source: a short neighbourhood/workshop notice with registration deadline, location, start time, one item to bring and a transport suggestion.
- Listening update: venue entrance changes, start time moves, one item is now provided, and a bus/train disruption changes expected arrival.
- Reading questions: distinguish deadline, start time, required item and original plan.
- Listening questions: identify which details changed and which remain valid.
- Mediation writing: send a 4–6 sentence message to a friend who has not seen the update, relaying only the current information.
- Partner task: partner has one missing detail; learner asks a clarification question, explains one preference/reason, proposes the workable option and confirms the final meeting.

This review should include at least one deliberately irrelevant but plausible detail so success requires selecting useful information rather than copying everything.

## Targeted reinforcement after the final review

Only after the cumulative pack is built and tested, review these small adjustments:

1. Add or revise 2–3 production tasks so learners relay information from a source to another person.
2. Ensure one later task uses a short public announcement format.
3. Recheck `want` / `omdat` / `daarom` explanations for consistent word-order guidance.
4. Check formal/informal address consistency inside each scenario and message.
5. Review repeated lexical notes: keep deliberate recycling, but replace any duplicate note that adds no new form, meaning or use.
6. Perform a Dutch/Arabic editorial pass on all 32 lessons after these changes rather than before them.

## Completion decision

After the cumulative review and targeted reinforcement pass, authored A2 can be considered **content-complete for internal DutchFlow purposes** if:

- all 8 unit packs plus the final cumulative review pass structural/content validation;
- the final review requires reception, production, interaction and explicit relay/mediation;
- later listening includes at least one public-announcement style source;
- no new major domain gap is found during independent educator review;
- learner observation does not reveal a systematic failure that requires a new teaching sequence.

Even then, do not describe the learner as “CEFR A2 certified” or the course as officially CEFR validated. Real recorded audio, independent educator review and learner pilot observations remain separate release gates.
